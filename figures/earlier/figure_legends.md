**Figure 1 | Study concept and data.** **a**, The 10-g monofilament tests large-fibre sensation at three plantar sites; pain, burning and tingling are conveyed by small intraepidermal fibres (inset) that it does not assess. **b**, Group definitions by monofilament result and self-reported foot pain or tingling (DIQ120/130). n, unweighted analysis sample; %, survey-weighted prevalence among US adults ≥40 y (95% CI). **c**, Transfer test: a cross-fitted gradient-boosting model trained to separate painless neuropathy from reference adults scores groups it never saw. **d**, Cohort flow over calendar time: adults examined in NHANES 1999–2004 (1999–2002 development, 2003–04 held-out) by group (darker band, died by 2019) and their vital status on 31 Dec 2019 from National Death Index linkage; ribbon widths are proportional to numbers of adults. Biochemistry, blood count and body size were measured at the same visit.

**Figure 2 | A model trained only on exam-confirmed painless neuropathy recognises the hidden group.** **a**, Out-of-fold neuropathy-similarity scores (logit) by group: density, individual adults (rug), interquartile range and median; dashed line, reference median. **b**, Survey-weighted differences in mean score versus reference: for the main model, the normal confidence distribution of the estimate with 66% (thick) and 95% (thin) cluster-robust intervals; smaller symbols, a model without glycaemic features, adults without diabetes and the held-out 2003–04 cycle (model trained on 1999–2002). Pre-specified contrast, hidden minus back-pain control: 0.19 (0.13–0.26), P = 4.0e-08. **c**, UMAP embedding of the same biomarkers (visualisation only), shaded by mean score; contours enclose the densest 50% of each group; small satellite clusters (6.3% of adults) not shown. **d**, Mean SHAP contribution of the twelve largest drivers in the hidden group (teal) and the back-pain control (grey), each minus reference. **e**, Out-of-fold AUROC of each model: transfer models (painless neuropathy vs reference, blue), positive-unlabeled models (brown); dashed line, main model.

**Figure 3 | The hidden group shares the biomarker profile of exam-confirmed neuropathy.** **a**, Standardized mean difference (SMD) of each biomarker versus reference after adjustment for age, sex and race. Top, hidden group with 95% CI; coloured strip, biomarker domain; bubble heatmap, all groups, biomarkers ordered by the hidden group (colour, SMD; circle area, |SMD|; outlined, 95% CI excludes 0); right, number of biomarkers whose CI excludes 0. **b,c**, Each point is one biomarker, coloured by domain (grey bars, 95% CIs): hidden-group SMD against painless neuropathy (**b**) and the back-pain control (**c**); dashed line, identity; solid line and band, least-squares fit with 95% confidence band; labelled, biomarkers whose 95% CI excludes 0 in the hidden group (bold and outlined, also in the comparison group). Pearson r P values are nominal (biomarkers are correlated). **d**, Cosine similarity between group profiles (outlined column: similarity with the hidden group). **e**, Cosine similarity with the hidden-group profile recomputed after leaving out one biomarker domain at a time (n, biomarkers retained); grey bar, range across groups.

**Figure 4 | A positive-unlabeled model ranks normal-exam adults by neuropathy-like biology, and symptoms follow the ranking.** Normal-exam adults were split into deciles of the PU posterior, ranked within 10-year age band × sex. Symptoms were not model inputs. **a**, Foot pain/tingling and numbness. **b**, Negative-control symptoms. **c**, Deaths during follow-up to 2019 (crude). Shaded bands, Wilson 95% CIs; labels, decile 10/decile 1 ratio. **d**, Mean age and percentage female by decile, confirming that the ranking is not an age–sex gradient. **e**, Odds ratios per SD of the PU score in all cycles (confidence distribution with 66%/95% intervals) and in the held-out 2003–04 cycle (diamonds; model trained on 1999–2002); a larger tuned model gave foot pain/tingling OR 1.66, back pain 1.17 and hand-only 1.05. Cox hazard ratios for mortality without and with ankle–brachial index, claudication and cardiovascular history.

**Figure 5 | Mortality over 17 years of linked follow-up.** **a**, Survey-weighted Kaplan–Meier survival by group (shaded, 95% CI) with unweighted numbers at risk and deaths/n. **b**, Survey-weighted Cox hazard ratios versus reference (confidence distribution on the log scale with 66%/95% intervals), adjusted for age, sex, race, education, income, smoking, BMI, diabetes and kidney function; 95% CIs from cluster-robust variance; deaths/n in the model sample. E-value for the hidden group 1.67 (CI limit 1.26). **c**, Cause-specific hazard ratios with 95% CIs (* P < 0.05, ** P < 0.01, *** P < 0.001), tiles coloured by log hazard ratio (blue < 1 < red); outlined and bold, 95% CI excludes 1. The hidden-group excess was not separable from peripheral vascular status (Fig. 6).

**Figure 6 | Sensitivity analyses as specification curves.** Each column is one pre-specified analysis, ordered by its estimate (thin line connects the ordered estimates); the dot matrix below shows which analytic choices it makes (shaded column, main analysis). **a**, Pre-specified biological contrast, hidden minus back-pain control (out-of-fold logit score). **b**, Adjusted hazard ratio for the hidden group versus reference with the back-pain control alongside (grey); red choices mark analyses that exclude, adjust for or restrict on peripheral circulation (also marked by triangles in both panels). Thick and thin bars, 66% and 95% CIs; hollow symbols, 95% CI includes the null; dashed line, main estimate. P values for each analysis are printed between the curve and the choice matrix.
